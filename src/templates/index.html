What is Anemia?

Anemia is a medical condition in which you lack sufficient healthy red blood cells to carry adequate oxygen to your body's tissues. It can cause fatigue, weakness, shortness of breath, and dizziness.

Common Types:
  • Iron Deficiency Anemia — Most common type
  • Vitamin Deficiency — B12 or folate shortage
  • Chronic Disease Anemia — Long-term conditions
  • Hemolytic Anemia — RBC destruction
Risk Factors:
  • Poor nutrition or dietary restrictions
  • Heavy bleeding or blood loss
  • Bone marrow disorders
  • Chronic kidney disease

Understanding Your Results

Results are displayed as a percentage representing the likelihood of anemia based on similar historical cases:

0-25% Low Risk

Patient profile is similar to non-anemic cases

25-50% Moderate Risk

Patient shows some anemia-like characteristics

50-75% High Risk

Strong indicators of anemia; further testing recommended

75-100% Very High Risk

Urgent medical evaluation and laboratory confirmation needed

Clinical Guidelines

CBC Parameters Analyzed
  • Hemoglobin (Hb): Protein that carries oxygen in red blood cells
  • Hematocrit (Hct): Percentage of red blood cells in blood
  • Red Blood Cell Count (RBC): Number of RBCs per unit volume
  • Mean Corpuscular Volume (MCV): Average size of RBCs
  • Mean Corpuscular Hemoglobin (MCH): Average hemoglobin per RBC
  • MCHC: Average hemoglobin concentration in RBCs
When to Seek Further Testing
  • HEMOSCAN indicates high or very high risk
  • Patient presents with symptoms like persistent fatigue
  • Unexplained decline in previous blood counts
  • Family history of blood disorders
  • Patient on medications known to affect RBC production
  • Chronic disease or pregnancy (requires specialized assessment)
